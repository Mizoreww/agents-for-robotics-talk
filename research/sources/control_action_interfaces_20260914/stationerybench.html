GPT-6 Astra vs MolmoAct2 on bimanual robotic manipulation

September 10, 2026

By Zihan Jack Zhang, Sravanthi Machcha, Sabrina Zou, Tzu Kit Chan, and Jay Chooi

StationeryBench is five bimanual manipulation tasks built from everyday desk stationery: a marker, a ruler, a lidded box, a stack of sticky-note pads, and a box of paper clips. We gave OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra, driven by the Inspect Robots agent policy, and Ai2's MolmoAct2, a vision-language-action model, control of the same YAM arms on all 5 tasks, 200 trials in total.

Scoring

Every trial was scored by a human grader on a 0 to 100 progress score: four milestones worth 25 points each, so 100 means the instruction was fully satisfied. The milestones are the same shape for every task and each task section below gives its own specifications. Using “Uncap the marker” as the example:

0Nothing purposeful. “Uncap the marker”: No purposeful contact with the marker.
25Deliberate contact with the target object(s). “Uncap the marker”: Touches or moves the marker deliberately.
50A secure grasp. “Uncap the marker”: Holds the marker securely in one gripper.
75The task's core manipulation achieved. “Uncap the marker”: Cap separated from the body, either part still held.
100The instruction fully satisfied. “Uncap the marker”: Cap and body both released on the table.

75Ruler held by the other gripper after a mid-air handover
100Ruler standing in the right cup and released

Astra completed 1 of 20 trials (mean progress 46 of 100) against MolmoAct2's 0 of 20 (16).

Completion bars have Wilson 95% confidence intervals. Dots show individual trial progress scores; diamonds show means with t 95% confidence intervals.

Pull the middle sticky note from a stack

25Touches or moves a pad deliberately

100Middle pad on the table, top pad resting on a pad and not on the table

Technical specifications

BenchmarkStationeryBench: 5 tasks, each with its own 0-4 rubric. Instructions and rubrics are listed per task above
EmbodimentBimanual I2RT YAM arms, 6-DoF per arm with parallel-jaw grippers
ControlAstra: absolute end-effector poses via the agent's tools. MolmoAct2: joint-space action chunks at 10 Hz from its /act server
ObservationThree camera views (top, left wrist, right wrist) plus proprioceptive state, frames are 224×224 policy inputs
PoliciesGPT-6 Astra: agent policy, medium effort, 20-LLM-call budget, 25% speed cap, 900-step cap, eef_pos interface, responses wire API; MolmoAct2: molmoact2 policy, action_horizon 30, num_steps not recorded, server_url not recorded, 1200-step cap
HarnessInspect Robots 0.58.0
Trials20 per model per task
Token counts / costAstra only, list price without caching discounts. GPT-6 Astra: $10 / $50 per million input / output tokens

Limitations

  • Grading was operator-judged with the model known, so scores are open to unconscious bias.
  • MolmoAct2 was run zero-shot, with the released allenai/MolmoAct2-BimanualYAM checkpoint, and no fine-tuning on these tasks or objects, which are not known to be in its training. The same checkpoint, server and rigs successfully complete its own pick-and-place tasks.
  • In 47 of 100 MolmoAct2 trials, the arms never left the start pose (commanded joint range under 1 rad over the entire run), including every trial of Pull the middle sticky note from a stack and Open the box, take out an eraser, close the lid. We attribute this to the scenes (one small object on a bare table) lying outside its training distribution rather than to the pipeline.
  • The two models were not always run on the same rig.Instructions are 135 to 297 characters which are longer than the short commands MolmoAct2 was trained on.
  • Objects were reset by hand between trials.
  • Astra was evaluated at medium effort only.
  • Frames are 224×224 policy inputs, so run videos are low resolution. Thinking pauses are removed from the videos.
