I kept seeing GPT-6 modelling results on here and got curious enough to build the thing myself: an agentic pipeline that turns a plain RGB video into 3D assets. The living room for robots MIT Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory (CSAIL). Input: one handheld phone walkthrough of our lab kitchen (~20s). Nothing else — no depth sensor, no CAD, no asset library. ~1 day (I actually slept overnight), including human-in-the-loop. Live: https://lnkd.in/e6N_UQhK Overall, I think the result is really good—better than I expected. It is still not perfect — thin and shiny things are still weak, a few objects are drafts, the room shell needs another pass. The loop, roughly: - Monocular video → metric scan (ViPE): camera poses + depth. This is the measuring instrument, not the output. - GPT-6 lists what should exist as separate objects, then open-vocabulary detection + tracking gives per-object masks; each object is fused and measured in metres. - For every asset, GPT-6 works in its own sandbox with tools: it writes the object as a program in a small Blender DSL (closed solids, PBR colours, hinges/drawers), builds it, renders it over the original video frames, compares it with the scan points in 3D, and iterates. - A separate GPT-6 session is the verifier — it can render the model on any frame it wants and must justify every complaint with a frame. The modeller never grades its own work. - A completeness pass renders the whole modelled scene from the video's own cameras, puts it next to the real frames, and says what's still missing. What the detector keeps missing (a row of identical cabinets) gets placed geometrically instead. - Out comes MJCF/URDF with joints. Now adding simulation to this env.

If your firm's AI strategy is still focused on using ChatGPT to write faster emails, you are missing the revolution.     saying “AGI is just the model” feels like saying “humans are just a brain”. I've been thinking about this watching the gpt-6 astra debate... a dude on reddit declared astra DEFINITELY NOT AGI because it couldn't create a convincing "cheetah running through the savannah using the Blender 3d software". and... the result wasn't great. but what exactly failed here? 1. the model's reasoning? 2. its ability to use Blender? 3. the I/O between the two? those are all different failures... and lumping them together doesn't tell us much about intelligence. for humans to demonstrate intelligence, we need experience, memory, feedback, tools... and often other humans to get things done. I think AI needs the equivalent: harness, context, memory, the right tools, verification loops, and often subagents. that's a system. and a failed task tells us that the system fell short. but we need to study and understand exactly where it fell short - as it might not be a question of intelligence + the model, but a limitation of the tools, context, or inputs/outputs available to it. I suspect AGI will emerge from the combination of model + environment - not just the model alone. 🔗 🐆 underwhelming cheetah: https://lnkd.in/gthnG_xg

    🚨 GPT-6 Astra just simulated an entire... human foot to explain why someone was having foot pain. 🤯 A guy named Emanuel_Andre7 on X was dealing with ankle pain, and instead of just Googling it, he asked GPT-6 Astra to help him understand what might be going on. The result? The model built a full interactive 3D atlas with bones, ligaments, tendons, real movement axes, sliders for both plantarflexion and inversion, and live visualization showing exactly what kind of force each ligament was under. One session. One question. Done. I was genuinely impressed reading this, but what gave me even more chills was the mechanism behind it. Astra did not generate an image. It wrote code. It used an extended reasoning budget to produce thousands of lines of WebGL and Three.js, generate accurate 3D geometry, and then render something you can rotate, drag, and interact with like a real app. And remember, on BenchCAD it reportedly reached 95.9% geometric overlap, far ahead of Claude Fable 5.1 at 84.3%. That whole area of “AI understanding 3D space” that we used to think belonged mostly to models from Google or Anthropic? Astra is catching up very fast. 🥶 I think what is changing here is not just that models are getting smarter. It is that when a model can both code and build an entire interactive 3D world, the vibe for coders starts shifting from writing components to something closer to actually “touching” and manipulating everything. Now people are starting to call a model when they have foot pain instead of immediately going to a doctor. The real question is: are these 3D atlases accurate enough to actually trust, or are they just visually convincing enough to make us feel like, “Ohhh, so that’s what’s happening”? What do you guys think? In a few years, could models like this actually replace parts of what physical therapists or rehabilitation doctors do? 👇

    Two frontier models released in the last few weeks: Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra. Both are SOTA, but they're positioned differently. I tried both. Short version: Fable is a researcher/strategist; Astra is a senior engineer/operator. Astra is good at using the computer to do day-to-day work. I asked it to review my web app. It went through the whole thing and produced a full report without stopping to ask. The community is already posting 3D CAD workflows, Blender scenes for games, and a digital copy of Manhattan. Since it operates software through the screen, keyboard, and mouse, that closes the gap for every application that hasn't built a mature MCP interface yet. For analysis and critical or creative thinking, I still go to Fable. I asked both models to review the same marketing blog post. Both reports were good. I gave both the same marketing blog to review, and both returned solid reports. Fable gave insight I hadn't considered; Astra applied standard criteria thoroughly. My take on AGI: it probably won't be one model. It will be a set of models, each good at different things, working together, which makes model routing a question of fit, not just cost. Same as a human organization. Put the right person in the right place. There's a Chinese line I like for this, 用人如器，各取所長: use people the way you use tools, each for what it does best. Caveat: personal read from a handful of tasks, not a benchmark. #AI #Astra #Fable

    Put people in front of specialized software they don’t use day to day, give them a set of tasks, and they will get through about 80% of them. That was our human baseline when we built GauntletBench in June. The best computer-use agent at the time solved 28.2% of the same tasks. The recently released GPT-6 Astra now scores 83%. We didn't expect the gap between human and agent performance to close that fast. Now the benchmark itself needs to catch up. We are already working on the next version of the benchmark, and that is the part I am looking forward to most. Grateful to Adel Bibi for guiding this from the beginning, and everyone else on the team. A great group to work with. #agents #benchmarking #gpt6 #agi #rnd
    Adel Bibi

    Senior Researcher in Machine Learning at the University of Oxford. R&D Distinguished Advisor at Softserve. AI Consultant and Advisor.

    let me tell you about my recent experience with gpt-6 astra.   in june, we released gauntletbench (link to paper/applciations/datasets in comments), a benchmark designed to test computer-use agents on VERY challenging and less familiar applications, including video editing, workflow building, 3d modelling, flight analysis, and circuit design. we did not advertise the paper as it was under review. at the time, the best-performing computer-use agent achieved 28.2%, compared with 80.1% for humans.   when astra was released recently, we thought we should test it on gauntletbench. it achieved 83%. this is the first agent we evaluated that surpasses the human baseline, and a jump in capability that i personally did not expect to see for at least another year (it was achieved in 3 months). the attached video shows astra solving one of our flight analyser tasks. the task was: “determine which flight is the last to exit a radius of 40 km around Karlovy Vary airport.”   the result is obviously very exciting, but it also says something important about how quickly benchmarks can become outdated. gauntletbench was specifically designed around the capability frontier only three months ago.   we are already working on gauntletbench 2.0. we will make the tasks more natural and goal-oriented, rather than providing a detailed sequence of steps; focus on the extremely difficult cases that remain solvable by humans; and continuously add new tasks as part of an online benchmark and leaderboard. the work was led by Mykola Vysotskyi and Runqi Lin with fantastic set of authors over industry SoftServe and academia. the updated paper, benchmark, code, tasks, and astra evaluation are available in the first comment:
